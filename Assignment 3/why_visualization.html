a) There is a difference in the mean number of steps between women and men.

c) The correlation coefficient between steps and bmi is positive for men.

The analysis of the dataset revealed several key findings. A significant difference in the mean number of steps between men and women was observed, with a p-value of approximately 1.32e-08, indicating that gender plays a role in step count. A negative correlation between steps and BMI was found for women, with a highly significant p-value of 2.81e-14, suggesting that women with higher BMI tend to take fewer steps. In contrast, for men, a positive correlation between steps and BMI was observed, with a p-value of 2.75e-13, indicating that men with higher BMI tend to take more steps. These results provide valuable insights into how gender and BMI influence physical activity patterns.
